\section{Fixing the implications in advance}
\label{sec:prereg-worked}

The implications derived from the theory were fixed in writing before the observations, so
that support and rejection could not be chosen afterwards. Twenty-two were registered: six
supported, eleven rejected, five untestable. The wording and the verdicts are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}.

\textbf{The number of rejections is not a failure of the method.} Without the fixing, an
after-the-fact account could have been given for any fact observed. Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}
records four places where a post-hoc interpretation was nearly adopted.

\section{Open problems}

The open problems are organized by the six categories of
Section~\ref{sec:obstacle-taxonomy} (Section~\ref{sec:sixth-obstacle}). Because the remedy
differs by category, the allocation of effort depends on the distinction.

\subsection{(i) No measurement exists}

The quantity is not measured anywhere. Secondary data will not fill the gap; an original
survey is required.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{Is directors' remuneration a cost or an appropriation of profit?}
The cause of the low $m$ in the small classes is directors' remuneration
(Proposition~\ref{prop:officer}). As a fraction of sales it is fifty times that of large
firms, and adding it back to operating profit removes the ordering by size.

The remaining question is whether that remuneration is a return to labour or a discretionary
appropriation. That the remuneration ratio does not move when profit does, and that the
operating margin of unincorporated businesses rises in a downturn
(Remark~\ref{rem:covid-reversal}), suggest discretion; but the labour-return account cannot
be rejected. Deciding requires directors' hours worked, and
\textbf{directors are not employees under the Labour Standards Act, so no official statistics
on their hours exist} (Remark~\ref{rem:officer-undecidable}).

An alternative route would identify from changes in the corporate tax rate or the thresholds
for reduced rates as exogenous shocks, but cutting a sample at a threshold requires
microdata.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{(ii) Ill-posed hypothesis}

The theory does not fix the sign. More data does not help; the theory must be refined.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),start=2]
\item \textbf{Testing the measurability hypothesis.}
The formula for $b^\star$ itself cannot be tested because the required quantities are not
available (Section~\ref{sec:what-to-measure}).

A test on the price side was carried out. Across 307 firms, fee rates achieved and separation
rates were uncorrelated (Section~\ref{sec:predX-result}). Separation rates differ 5.5-fold
across occupational averages while fee rates differ only 1.16-fold, and prices cluster on the
customary 20--30\%.

The obstacle is therefore not only (ii). It belongs to (vi) of
Section~\ref{sec:sixth-obstacle} --- \textbf{the explanatory variable does not vary over the
range the theory assumes} --- and is resolved neither by refining the theory nor by adding
data.

\item \textbf{The objective under R2: are $H$ and $\beta$ enough?}
A route was shown that explains the change through $H$ (time horizon) and $\beta$ (discount
factor) without changing the form of the objective (Section~\ref{sec:r2-sst}). Whether that
suffices is untested.

Testing requires observing contract choices among older people, and no distribution of
contract-form choice by age is available. The supply side's price design is consistent with
the alternative, but it is not consumers' choice.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{(iii) Not identified}

The data exist but confounding cannot be separated. Better design or exogenous variation is
required.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),start=4]
\item \textbf{Interpreting the lengthening of receivables.}
The long-run rise in $\CCC$ was decomposed in Section~\ref{sec:ccc-decomposition}. Changes in
industrial composition are excluded (the composition effect is 17\%, and 2\% since 2010), and
the rise in the 2000s is explained by shorter DPO as trade bills disappeared.

What remains is the lengthening of receivables in the 2010s ($+6.62$ days). Whether this is a
genuine lengthening of collection or a reclassification accompanying the shift to
electronically recorded monetary claims cannot be separated. Separating them requires
balances of such claims, which the corporate statistics do not survey
(Remark~\ref{rem:densai}).

\item \textbf{What drives the variation in $\Delta\ln W$.}
The contemporaneous negative correlation was separated in
Section~\ref{sec:denominator-effect}, giving a denominator-effect contribution of about 17\%.
That the denominator effect is not dominant could be shown.

An estimate allowing for lag structure was attempted but nothing was detectable in annual
data (Remark~\ref{rem:lag-undetectable}). The $R^2$ of the elasticity estimate is only 0.081,
so most of the annual variation in working capital is driven by something other than sales.
Year-end inventory adjustment, timing shifts on large transactions, and changes in accounting
policy plausibly contaminate it, but none is separated.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{(iv) Access constraint}

It exists but cannot be obtained. A route must be opened, or the attempt abandoned.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),start=6]
\item \textbf{Measuring $\phi_{\rm cog}$.}
Of the three conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:phicog-requirements}, only (1), the
contractual right, was reached here. (2), individual realized use, and (3), an exogenous
event forcing attention, are missing.

A detour was constructed through $\delta^{*}=F/p$, but it applies only to binary menus and
the proxy is not uniquely determined for a stepped tariff (Remark~\ref{rem:detour-limit}).

For family 2-4 (prepaid), \cite{nber2026pnbl} shows that about 40\% of prepaid value goes
unused, so \textbf{the quantity this text could not measure for family 2 has already been
measured}. The remaining gaps are an estimate for family 2-5 (options) and a cross-industry
estimate of the share of $\phi_{\rm cog}$ within total $\phi$.

\item \textbf{An indicator of how readily surplus is taken.}
No indicator corresponding to the horizontal axis of the two in Chapter~\ref{part:industry}
could be constructed. The reason is structural.

\textbf{An intermediary is an entity that spans industries, not an attribute of one}
(Remark~\ref{rem:no-horizontal}). It cannot in principle be captured by industry aggregates.
Attempting a cross-section on the intermediary side fails because rates decided by individual
screening are not disclosed.
\textbf{That a rate is negotiated presupposes that nobody outside the transaction knows it.}
Collecting only published rates biases the sample towards thin intermediaries operating from
a price list.

An implication was registered for the risk component (Section~\ref{sec:pred-J}), but the data
needed to estimate $\rho$ lie inside intermediaries, and industries with high $\rho$ are
screened out, so it cannot be tested.

\item \textbf{Curvature of the age effect.}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:apc} the levels of the three coefficients are not identified, but
the second difference --- the curvature --- is (Remark~\ref{rem:apc-identified}).

\textbf{Pursuing this requires a sample carrying firm age.} The corporate statistics used
here have three axes --- industry, size and year --- and do not include the year of founding.
As Remark~\ref{rem:apc-in-this-work} states, an age effect is latent in the $\CCC$ time
series, and there is no means of separating it.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Matters of the framework's descriptive range}

Items that do not fit the taxonomy of obstacles and concern the setting of the object itself.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),start=9]
\item \textbf{The simplification of the capacity constraint.}
As Remark~\ref{rem:cap-simplification} states, $\mathrm{Cap}$ was treated as a constant.
Section~\ref{sec:cap-observation} records an observation with which this does not agree, but
with $n=1$ the theory was not amended (Remark~\ref{rem:no-generalization}). Whether the same
limit appears in other families is unconfirmed.

\item \textbf{Social optimality of pooling.}
This text is written from the standpoint of an individual operator and does not treat the
effect of the choice of $\Phi$ on the system (Remark~\ref{rem:pooling-social}). Families 5-4
(underwriting) and 6-2 (escrow) make pooling their business, and pooling that is individually
optimal may be excessive for the system.

\item \textbf{The credit position of sole proprietors.}
No published statistic measures the $\kappa$ of sole proprietors. The Survey of Unincorporated
Enterprises dropped its assets and liabilities items in FY2019, and the Basic Survey on Small
and Medium Enterprises has a questionnaire for sole proprietors but does not tabulate assets
and liabilities (Section~\ref{sec:not-tabulated}).

As a result, the effect of the Freelance Act (in force November 2024) setting an upper bound
on $\tau$ cannot be tested. Against the three forms of the institutional layer organized in
Section~\ref{sec:institution-mode}, this is \textbf{a fourth form that regulates time
directly}, and it is of high theoretical interest as a case in which law imposes a ceiling on
the $\tau_i$ of Section~\ref{sec:tau}.

\item \textbf{Forms in which claims circulate.}
The $\Phi:\Delta\to\Pi$ of Chapter~\ref{sec:phi} presumes that delivery and settlement
correspond between the same counterparty. When settlement is resold to a third party, as with
token issuance, the party receiving delivery changes after the fact and cannot be expressed
in a setting with $N$ fixed (Section~\ref{sec:token-limit}). An extension of the domain is
required.

\item \textbf{Observation through protocol records. The tests were carried out.}
They were carried out as reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:protocol-emp}. This was the first test of
the deductions of Part~\ref{part:catalog}, and only $\Phi$2 was supported.

\textbf{What remains is per-party quantities.} What is observable is per identifier, and
because one party may hold several identifiers the per-party $\kappa_i$ cannot be recovered
(Remark~\ref{rem:additivity}). The obstacle is not the absence of aggregation but
\textbf{its impossibility}, and it differs from both forms added in
Section~\ref{sec:sixth-obstacle}. The tests revealed a second form of this divergence: the
concentration of identifiers pointing to one implementation
(Remark~\ref{rem:clone-concentration}).

What could be verified, moreover, is the correctness of a deduction, not whether that
deduction applies to real solo businesses (Remark~\ref{rem:protocol-population}).

This is the same place as item (12): a constraint originating in the setting of the index.

\item \textbf{A quantity expressing a degree of unmannedness.}
``How unmanned is it?'' cannot be expressed as a ratio. Measured on the delivery side, the
valuation map $v$ measures only value; measured on the response side, no measure is defined
on $\Omega\setminus\hat\Omega$ (Remark~\ref{rem:automation-rate-illposed}). The obstacle is
the absence of a measure, not of a unit, and sharpening the definition does not remove it.

\item \textbf{Separating $\phi_{\rm prod}$ from $\phi_{\rm barg}$.}
Definition~\ref{def:phi-prod} requires the price $p^{\ast}$ that would obtain without market
power, which is a counterfactual and is not observed
(Remark~\ref{rem:counterfactual-price}). The numerical separation achieved in
Chapter~\ref{sec:platform-fee} rests on the exceptional availability of a within-platform
comparison differing only in function. For a general object, an additional assumption
identifying $p^{\ast}$ is required.

\item \textbf{The degree to which the third party is removed.}
Chapter~\ref{ch:protocol} treats only the cases where the protocol bears recording and
enforcement of settlement entirely and not at all, not the middle
(Remark~\ref{rem:oracle}). The composition of the party writing external facts into the state
varies, and most existing arrangements are in the middle.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Items with no obstacle}

The following fall under none of the obstacles of Section~\ref{sec:obstacle-taxonomy}. Either
the route of acquisition is established or the work can be done with data already obtained.
They have simply not been started.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),start=17]
\item \textbf{Decomposing $\lambda_{\rm novel}$ into components.}
Section~\ref{sec:protocol-byproducts} measured the response rates: 0.048 a year for
modification and 0.030 for cessation, so the $1/\lambda_{\rm novel}$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:unmanned-period} is shorter than 20 years.

\textbf{What remains is the decomposition into components.} What was measured is the total of
responses, without separating whether they originate in vulnerabilities, dependencies,
institutions or demand. Separating them requires reading the content of each modification,
which is possible only where verified sources exist, so the sample is biased.

\item \textbf{Correcting the scope of inventory.}
Chapter~\ref{part:industry} uses only ``finished goods and merchandise'' and omits
work in progress and raw materials, so $\mathrm{DIO}$ is understated.

\textbf{This was carried out for 62 industries} (Section~\ref{sec:inventory-scope}). The
median $\CCC$ moves from 28.0 to 43.9 days, but the correlation of $\Delta\CCC$ is 0.882 and
the denominator effect moves only from 0.160 to 0.172. The chapter's conclusions stand.

What remains is the cross-section with size classes. ``Inventory (period end)'' is available
for 62 industries only for all sizes together, and by size class for only 10 industries.
\textbf{A cross-section split by both industry and size exceeds e-Stat's 100{,}000-cell
limit.} It is possible by splitting the retrieval, but what would be obtained is information
linking the two cross-sections of Remark~\ref{rem:inventory-cut}, which would not move the
chapter's conclusions.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Known weaknesses of the framework}

The weaknesses revealed through verification fall into three types. None is an error in an
individual proposition; all \textbf{originate in the construction of the framework}.

\subsection{(B) Excessive assumptions of exogeneity}

Institutions, $\Phi$, $v$, $\mathrm{Cap}$ and $\mathcal{G}$ are all treated as exogenous.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{Subjectivity of the valuation map $v$.}
The $v$ that measures delivery in money was taken as given, but it is itself subjective, and
the definition of $\phi_{\mathrm{cog}}$ depends on it.

\item \textbf{Endogeneity of $\Phi$, and switching cost.}
$\Phi$ was treated as a firm's choice variable, but it is in fact an equilibrium of
bargaining between parties and cannot be chosen unilaterally. In addition the switching cost
is set to zero (Remark~\ref{rem:switching-cost}). Where that cost is of the same order as the
gain in surplus, waiting has value and a question of timing arises --- when to fix $\Phi$.
This text does not treat it.

\item \textbf{Exogeneity of the institutional layer.}
Institutions were treated only as an exogenous constraint. In fact they change endogenously in
response to firm behaviour. The correspondence between antitrust litigation and rate
reductions in Chapter~\ref{sec:platform-fee} is an example.

\item \textbf{Simplification of $\mathrm{Cap}$.}
It was treated as a constant, but it is properly a function of time and is also a design
variable through restrictions on hours of use (Remark~\ref{rem:cap-simplification}).

\item \textbf{Endogeneity of $\mathcal{G}$.}
The information structure was taken as given, but an operator can extend it through follow-up
surveys (Remark~\ref{rem:G-endogenous}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{(C) Level and change are not separated}
\label{sec:level-change-gap}

\textbf{This is the weakness most easily missed.} (A) and (B) can be recognized as
``the assumption is too strong'', whereas (C) has no corresponding concept in the theory and
is hard to notice as an absence.

As Remark~\ref{rem:level-vs-change} states, every quantity here is defined as a level. Yet in
the empirical work, four times over, cross-sectional comparison of levels could not be
interpreted and only change could be discussed.

\textbf{The theory has only levels while the empirical work can speak only of change.}

Part of this mismatch was resolved by introducing the lag $\tau_i$ in
Section~\ref{sec:tau}. Proposition~\ref{prop:ccc-structure} gives
$\CCC=\sum_i\tau_i+\tau_{\mathrm{inv}}$, from which it is deduced that the level of $\CCC$ is
fixed by $\Phi$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:ccc-level}) and that cross-sectional comparison is
meaningless (Corollary~\ref{cor:no-cross-comparison}). A tool for splitting $\Delta\CCC$ into
change in contracts and change in operations was also obtained
(Corollary~\ref{cor:ccc-change}).

That level and change are separate constraints was likewise settled at the boundary of the
fundamental inequality of Proposition~\ref{prop:fundamental}: $M\geq 0$ bounds the level and
$\dot M\geq 0$ bounds change (Remark~\ref{rem:level-and-change}). The two are independent,
yet their sensitivity to $\tau_i$ carries the same coefficient $-1/\CCC$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:tau-substitution}, Table~\ref{tab:comparative-statics}).

\textbf{What remains is constructing a comparable quantity.} The candidate is the deviation
from the contractual $\tau_i^{\mathrm{contract}}$, but contractual terms do not exist in
published statistics (Remark~\ref{rem:no-normalization}). The theoretical problem is solved
and the measurement problem has taken its place.
