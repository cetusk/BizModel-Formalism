\section{Why a taxonomy is needed}

``Cannot be verified'' covers states of different kinds. Because the remedies differ, they
must be treated separately. This taxonomy is an organization contributed by this text.

When work stalls at stage 3 of the five stages of Chapter~\ref{ch:design}, availability,
one cannot decide whether to spend effort or abandon the attempt without being able to say
what exactly is stuck.

\section{Six categories}
\label{sec:obstacle-taxonomy}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.30\textwidth}X@{}}
\toprule
Category & State & Correct remedy \\
\midrule
(i) No measurement exists & nobody anywhere measures the quantity & conduct an original survey; secondary data will never fill the gap \\
(ii) Ill-posed hypothesis & the theory does not fix the sign & refine the theory; more data does not help \\
(iii) Not identified & the data exist but confounding cannot be broken & better design; find exogenous variation or a control group \\
(iv) Access constraint & it exists but cannot be obtained & open a route, or abandon on legal or technical grounds \\
(v) A choice in aggregation & it is surveyed but does not appear in the published tables & request the tabulation, or apply to use anonymized microdata \\
(vi) The adjustment does not operate & data are ample, but the explanatory variable does not vary over the range the theory assumes & \textbf{no route resolves it} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\captionof{table}{Six categories of verification obstacle: the state and the correct remedy.}
\label{tab:obstacle-taxonomy}
\label{sec:sixth-obstacle}
\end{center}

\textbf{The role of the taxonomy is to separate what effort can move from what it cannot.}

(iii), (iv) and (v) can move with effort or ingenuity: (iii) with design, (iv) with a route,
(v) with a change in what is published.

(i), (ii) and (vi) do not move. (i) cannot be filled without bringing the measurement into
existence; (ii) does not fix a sign however much data is added; and under (vi), so long as
the explanatory variable does not vary, no increase in sample size makes a test possible.

\begin{remark}[(vi) is not a negative result]
\label{rem:vi-is-observation}
(vi) is a defect neither of the theory nor of the measurement. It is
\textbf{the fact that the adjustment mechanism the theory assumes does not operate in
reality}.

That a theoretical prediction fails to appear is itself an observation. In
Part~\ref{part:results} the statement ``fee rates are not adjusted to the risk of the
outcome'' is such a case (Remark~\ref{rem:no-adjustment}).

In terms of the layers of Section~\ref{sec:layers}, prices being set by custom is a
phenomenon of the institutional layer. The picture is one in which a theory premised on
optimization fails to operate in the face of custom.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Impossibility of aggregation differs from a choice in aggregation]
\label{rem:agg-impossible}
Under (v) the quantity is merely not tabulated; it is in the sample. By contrast, there are
cases where the unit of observation does not correspond to a party, so a quantity per party
cannot be recovered. The identifiers of Chapter~\ref{ch:protocol} are such a case
(Remark~\ref{rem:additivity}).

That case is not resolved by a change in what is published. It is closer to (i) than to (v),
but differs in that the quantity is not unmeasured;
\textbf{the unit of measurement differs from the unit of the question}.
\end{remark}

\section{The taxonomy also serves as a test before starting}

The taxonomy organizes, after the fact, why a verification did not complete; it also
\textbf{serves as a test before a survey is begun}.

At stage 3 of Chapter~\ref{ch:design} it is the criterion for judging whether the quantity
can be obtained at the granularity required. The feasibility assessment of
Chapter~\ref{ch:platformfee} is a worked instance of this use.
