\chapter{Evidence From Protocol Records: \texorpdfstring{$M(t)$}{M(t)} and the Rate of Modification}
\label{ch:protocol-emp}
%============================================================

In the ``inside'' partition of Chapter~\ref{ch:protocol}, $M(t)$, $\kappa_j$, $\dbar-\delta$
and $B$ are published per identifier (Section~\ref{sec:protocol-observability}). These are
quantities that other chapters cannot measure for want of per-firm data. This chapter
observes them.

The population is every $\Phi$ operating at the reference date, and the sample was not chosen
after the fact.

\section{The implications tested}
\label{sec:protocol-predictions}

Following the procedure of Chapter~\ref{ch:next}, four implications were fixed before
acquisition. They are numbered with $\Phi$ to distinguish them.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Implication $\Phi$1}: the fewer other procedures a procedure calls, the longer until
it is modified. This is the $\lambda_{\rm dep}$ side of
Proposition~\ref{prop:unmanned-period}.

\textbf{Alternative}: the frequency of modification is fixed by usage, not by the number of
dependencies. Few dependencies but heavy use still leads to modification.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Implication $\Phi$2}: for a $\Phi$ whose transactions cease, $M(t)$ declines
monotonically before the cessation. This follows from $\tau=\inf\{t:M(t)<0\}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:objective} being absorbing.

\textbf{Alternative}: cessation occurs independently of $M(t)$, which does not change until
immediately before.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Implication $\Phi$3}: the distribution of $\CCC$ among $\Phi$ operating in the
``inside'' partition is skewed non-negative, because by
Corollary~\ref{cor:growth-needs-capital} realizing $\CCC<0$ requires $E>0$.

\textbf{Alternative}: the distribution of $\CCC$ resembles that of the corporate sector in
Chapter~\ref{part:industry}, with no skew specific to the ``inside'' partition.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Implication $\Phi$4}: assigning operating $\Phi$ by the rule of
Section~\ref{sec:type-assignment}, family 7 barely appears except for 7-4. This follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:beneficiary}.

\textbf{Alternative}: family 7 types appear with about the same weight as other families.
\end{quote}

\begin{remark}[$\lambda_{\rm dep}$ and usage can be correlated]
\label{rem:dep-usage-confound}
The alternative to implication $\Phi$1 is hard to reject, because procedures with many
dependencies tend to be feature-rich and also heavily used. The test must control for usage,
and whether an effect of dependencies survives in the residual cannot be known in advance.
\textbf{The control procedure is fixed before acquisition.}
\end{remark}

\label{sec:protocol-results}

One implication was supported.

$\Phi$3 & 52 operating $\Phi$ & the alternative is not identified & untestable \\

\begin{remark}[Concentration of clones dominates the estimate]
\label{rem:clone-concentration}
The 1.93 million identifiers point to only 76{,}609 distinct implementations, and 1.55
million of them --- 80\% of the population --- point to a single one. These are not
independent $\Phi$ but copies of one $\Phi$.

Over the whole sample the coefficient on the number of dependencies is positive and
significant ($p=0.004$), but removing that one cluster reverses the sign and destroys
significance ($p=0.45$).
\textbf{Cluster-robust standard errors do not protect against this.} They correct the
correlation within a cluster but not the bias in a point estimate when one cluster accounts
for 95\% of the exposure.

The loss of additivity in Remark~\ref{rem:additivity} concerned the divergence between
parties and identifiers; the concentration of identifiers pointing to one implementation is a
different form of divergence.
\textbf{When assembling a sample from a ledger, the concentration of clones must be counted
first.}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The alternative is not identified]
\label{rem:phi3-illposed}
$\CCC<0$ means payment in advance, which by Corollary~\ref{cor:growth-needs-capital} requires
$E>0$. But the $\CCC$ of the comparison group, the corporate sector, is non-negative in all
58 industries of Chapter~\ref{part:industry} (minimum 4.9 days). A negative $\CCC$ appears in
individual firms and cancels out on aggregation by industry.

The implication and the alternative therefore say the same thing, and no observation
distinguishes them. The ill-posedness has the same form as that of the measurability
hypothesis in Section~\ref{sec:predB-illposed}. In addition, $\tau$ in the ``inside''
partition is measured in seconds against days in the corporate sector --- five orders of
magnitude apart.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Being implementable and appearing are different]
\label{rem:implementable-not-observed}
Family 7 is 1 of 52, so the quantitative part --- that it is rare --- was right. But what
appeared was 7-2 (cross-subsidy), and 7-4 appeared zero times.

The implication derived ``only 7-4 remains'' from Proposition~\ref{prop:beneficiary}, but the
proposition speaks of whether implementation is possible, not of how often something appears.
\textbf{Deriving appearance from implementability was the error.}

The single 7-2 selects its beneficiaries by signature, outside the protocol. That is
consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:beneficiary}, but since the wording of the implication
missed, it is not treated as supporting evidence.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A step is needed before the assignment order]
\label{rem:is-it-phi}
Table~\ref{tab:family-assignment-order} takes $\Phi$ as given. Applied to ledger identifiers,
131 of a sample of 200 were not $\Phi$ at all: 74 were token ledgers (a medium of settlement
with no pair of $\delta$ and $\pi$), 27 were infrastructure with no consideration, and
\textbf{30 were identifiers imitating the name of a settlement medium}.

The last satisfy Definition~\ref{def:protocol} but have no delivery. The unobservability of
$\iota$ in Section~\ref{sec:identifier} appears here as misrepresentation of a name.
\end{remark}

\section{Quantities measurable from the same records}
\label{sec:protocol-byproducts}

Three quantities that other chapters could not measure can be measured from the records used
for these tests.

\subsection{The rate of response to unforeseen states}
Proposition~\ref{prop:unmanned-period} put the unmanned operating period at
$1/\lambda_{\rm novel}$. Two rates can be measured in the ``inside'' partition.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXll@{}}
\toprule
Quantity & Content & $\lambda$ (per year) & $1/\lambda$ \\
\midrule
Modification rate $\lambda_{\rm fix}$ & rate at which the implementation was replaced; 1.93m records, excluding the clone concentration & 0.048 & 20.6 years \\
Cessation rate $\lambda_{\rm stop}$ & rate at which operation ceased; all 1{,}718 & 0.030 & 33.8 years \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\captionof{table}{Response rates measured in the ``inside'' partition.}
\label{tab:lambda-measured}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}[What is measured is not $\lambda_{\rm novel}$]
\label{rem:lambda-is-response}
Both entries in Table~\ref{tab:lambda-measured} count \textbf{responses to arrivals}, not
\textbf{arrivals of unforeseen states}. A modification is a case where the operator
responded; a cessation is a case where they did not, or could not.

Arrivals that required no response appear in neither. Hence
\[
\lambda_{\rm novel}\ \geq\ \lambda_{\rm fix}+\lambda_{\rm stop}
\]
and what was measured is a lower bound. The two are not additive because their populations
differ, but an upper bound follows:
\textbf{the $1/\lambda_{\rm novel}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:unmanned-period} is shorter than
20 years}.

The distribution of time to modification is extremely skewed. The median of the 20{,}732 that
were modified is 42 days; the median of the 1.9 million that ended the observation without
modification is 4.6 years.
\textbf{What gets touched is touched at once; what does not goes untouched for years.}
A mean $1/\lambda$ conceals this bimodality.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Duration of the procedures}
Chapter~\ref{ch:platformfee} abandoned direct measurement of the unmanned operating period
for want of a population frame. As Section~\ref{sec:protocol-frame} notes, that reason
disappears in the ``inside'' partition.

Of all 1{,}718 that operated, 143 (8.3\%) ceased and 1{,}575 continue. Among those that
ceased, the median duration was 1.62 years, the quartiles 0.64--2.82 years, and the maximum
6.0 years. Those continuing have a median of 2.75 years. Because the ceased were counted from
the full population rather than collected after the fact, no conditioning on survival
(Section~\ref{sec:survivorship}) arises.

Cessation rates are ordered by kind: services $1/\lambda=11.1$ years, high-leverage operation
11.5, issuance support 15.3, indices 18.1, bridging 20.1, insurance 22.3, lending 22.6, and
yield 22.7. \textbf{The direction --- kinds with lighter dependence on other procedures last
longer} --- can be read off, but since implication $\Phi$1 was rejected, this ordering is not
explained by the number of dependencies.

\subsection{Locked state $B$}
Table~\ref{tab:protocol-quantities} recorded that this text has no quantity corresponding to
$B$. In the ``inside'' partition it is directly observable as assets placed on the protocol,
and daily series for 1{,}718 records are available. This is what served as the proxy for
$M(t)$ in implication $\Phi$2.

\subsection{Rates}
Once $B$ is measurable, a rate can be defined as annual fees divided by $B$. But
\textbf{the denominator differs by family}. For family 3 (renting assets) $B$ is the right
denominator, whereas for family 6 (intermediation) the denominator is transaction value and
$B$ is merely inventory. Mixing them makes the ratio diverge.

Restricting to family 3 types with $B$ of at least one million dollars, 144 records give the
following.
