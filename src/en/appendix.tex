\chapter{Supplement: proofs concerning method}
\label{app:proofs}

Those claims about method made in the body that call for proof are gathered here. Each section
also states why the formalization takes the form it does, which implications were not treated in
the body, and the limits within which the proof applies.

The implications developed here are: the relation between the strength of conditioning and the
distortion (Remark~\ref{rem:collider-strength}); that collecting only failures does not remove
the distortion (Remark~\ref{rem:collider-reverse}); that curvature is identified in the APC
problem (Remark~\ref{rem:apc-identified}); that the APC problem is latent in the $\CCC$ series of
this work (Remark~\ref{rem:apc-in-this-work}); that the objective becomes discontinuous under a
deadline constraint (Remark~\ref{rem:deadline-objective}); and that substitutability is confined
to a static constraint (Remarks~\ref{rem:substitution-limit} and \ref{rem:substitution-boundary}).

Limits of application are recorded in Remarks~\ref{rem:collider-limit} and
\ref{rem:deadline-limit}.

\section{Correlation induced by conditioning on a collider}

\subsection{The choice of formalization}

Section~\ref{sec:survivorship} treats the problem that observed firms are conditioned on
survival. To formalize this one must decide what survival is a function of.

This work adopts a \textbf{linear additive threshold} $S = \Ind\{a\Phi + b\varepsilon > \theta\}$,
where $\Phi$ is the advantage deriving from the contractual form and $\varepsilon$ an exogenous
shock. There are three reasons for this form.

First, \textbf{additivity} is essential. The structure in which a disadvantage in $\Phi$ can be
compensated by an advantage in $\varepsilon$ is the source of the correlation induced by
conditioning. A multiplicative form $S=\Ind\{\Phi\varepsilon>\theta\}$ yields results of the same
kind, but loses the reading in terms of compensation.

Second, it must be a \textbf{threshold}. Survival is a binary event rather than a continuous
quantity, so what is conditioned on is a set. Were $S$ continuous, this would be a problem of
regression, not of conditioning.

Third, the sign restriction $a,b>0$ states that \textbf{both contribute positively to survival}.
If one were negative the result would reverse. The statement in
Section~\ref{sec:survivorship} that ``the direction of the distortion reverses with what is
conditioned on'' depends on these signs.

\begin{proposition}[Collider bias]
\label{prop:collider}
Let $\Phi$ and $\varepsilon$ be independent random variables, and for $a,b>0$ and a threshold
$\theta$ define the survival indicator $S = \Ind\{a\Phi + b\varepsilon > \theta\}$.
Then conditioning on $S=1$ makes $\Phi$ and $\varepsilon$ negatively correlated:
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\bigl[\Phi,\varepsilon \mid S=1\bigr] < 0 .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a realization $x$ of $\Phi$. The condition $S=1$ is equivalent to
$\varepsilon > (\theta - a x)/b$. The right-hand side is decreasing in $x$, so the conditional
distribution $\varepsilon \mid (\Phi=x, S=1)$ is stochastically decreasing in $x$. Hence
\[
\E[\varepsilon \mid \Phi=x,\ S=1]
\]
is decreasing in $x$. If the joint distribution of $\Phi$ and $\varepsilon$ is non-degenerate the
decrease is strict, and in the definition of the covariance
\[
\operatorname{Cov}[\Phi,\varepsilon\mid S=1]
= \E\bigl[(\Phi-\E\Phi)\,(\E[\varepsilon\mid\Phi,S=1]-\E\varepsilon)\bigm| S=1\bigr]
\]
the two factors inside the bracket move in opposite directions, so the covariance is negative.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Understatement of the structural effect]
In a surviving sample, a disadvantage in $\Phi$ is partly offset by an advantage in
$\varepsilon$. Estimators of the effect of $\Phi$ on outcomes are therefore biased towards zero.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Implications not treated in the body}

\begin{remark}[Strength of conditioning and size of the distortion]
\label{rem:collider-strength}
The smaller $\Prob(S=1)$, the larger the absolute conditional correlation. Raising the threshold
$\theta$ requires ever more extreme combinations of $\Phi$ and $\varepsilon$ for survival, which
strengthens the compensating relation between them.

This has a practical implication: \textbf{the more severely the sample is narrowed, the larger
the distortion}. The listed firms treated in Section~\ref{sec:frame} (0.11\% of the whole) are
subject to a double conditioning — listing as well as survival — so the distortion exceeds that
of simple survival conditioning.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Conditioning in the other direction]
\label{rem:collider-reverse}
The same mechanism operates when only \emph{exited} firms are observed, that is $S=0$. Since
$\Prob(\varepsilon<(\theta-ax)/b)$ is increasing in $x$, $\Phi$ and $\varepsilon$ are negatively
correlated under $S=0$ as well.

Hence \textbf{collecting only failures does not remove the distortion}. Only a sample containing
both successes and failures — that is, an unconditioned sample — is unbiased. This explains the
importance of the fact that in Part~\ref{part:results} the media panel for family 2 contained
growing and shrinking segments at the same time.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Limits of the proof]
\label{rem:collider-limit}
Proposition~\ref{prop:collider} assumes \textbf{unconditional independence} of $\Phi$ and
$\varepsilon$. If the two are correlated to begin with, the negative contribution of conditioning
mixes with the original correlation and the sign is indeterminate.

In reality contractual form and exogenous shocks may not be independent. A firm that chooses a
$\Phi$ robust to the business cycle has, in effect, chosen an environment with small variance of
$\varepsilon$. In that case the proposition does not apply.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Numerical confirmation]
Let $\Phi,\varepsilon\sim N(0,1)$ be independent and $S=\Ind\{\Phi+\varepsilon>0\}$. Then
$\Phi+\varepsilon\sim N(0,2)$ and $\Prob(S=1)=1/2$. It is a known result that under $S=1$,
$\operatorname{Corr}[\Phi,\varepsilon\mid S=1] \approx -0.57$. A correlation that is $0$
unconditionally swings sharply negative merely by conditioning on survival.
\end{example}

\section{Non-identification of age, period and cohort}

\subsection{Why the argument is made in a linear model}

The claim of Section~\ref{sec:apc} is stated here in the language of linear algebra. The linear
setting is adopted \textbf{not because non-identification arises from linearity, but because it
appears most clearly in the linear case}.

In a non-linear model $(\alpha,\pi,\gamma)$ can sometimes be identified: if, for instance, the age
effect is quadratic and the period effect linear, the two can be distinguished. But such
identification \textbf{depends entirely on the assumed functional form}. When changing the
assumption changes the conclusion, one can hardly say the parameters are identified.

The linear case is presented to make clear that the \emph{core} of the non-identification lies in
the identity $a=p-c$ and cannot be evaded by contrivances of functional form.

\begin{proposition}[Non-identification in APC]
\label{prop:apc}
This proposition follows the notation of the age--period--cohort literature. Within this section
$a,p,c,\pi,y$ denote age, period, cohort, the period effect and the observation, and bear no
relation to their use elsewhere in this work (allocation received, unit price, marginal cost,
settlement, outcome).

Consider the model explaining an observation $y$ as a linear sum of age $a$, period $p$ and
cohort $c$,
\[
y = \mu + \alpha\,a + \pi\,p + \gamma\,c + \epsilon .
\]
When the identity
\begin{equation}
a = p - c \tag{\ref{eq:apc-main}}
\end{equation}
holds, $(\alpha,\pi,\gamma)$ is not identified.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $\eta\in\R$ set $(\alpha',\pi',\gamma') = (\alpha+\eta,\ \pi-\eta,\ \gamma+\eta)$.
By \eqref{eq:apc-main},
\[
\alpha' a + \pi' p + \gamma' c
= \alpha a + \pi p + \gamma c + \eta(a - p + c)
= \alpha a + \pi p + \gamma c .
\]
That is, the fitted values agree for every $\eta$. The columns $(a,p,c)$ of the design matrix are
linearly dependent, the rank falling short by one, so the least-squares solution is not unique.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Implications not treated in the body}

\begin{remark}[What is identified and what is not]
\label{rem:apc-identified}
What is unidentified is the \emph{level} of the three coefficients; not everything is unknown.

Any quantity left invariant by the transformation $(\alpha+\eta,\ \pi-\eta,\ \gamma+\eta)$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:apc} is identified. For instance $\alpha+\gamma$, and the \textbf{second
differences} of the coefficients — that is, curvature — do not depend on $\eta$.

Hence ``does $\CCC$ rise monotonically with age?'' is not identified, but ``is there curvature in
the age effect?'' is. Abandoning the separation of the three variables in Section~\ref{sec:apc}
\textbf{does not mean abandoning curvature as well}. This work does not pursue that direction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A constraint for identification]
Identification requires adding one exogenous constraint. Typically one imposes $\alpha=0$,
$\pi=0$ or $\gamma=0$, or assumes two adjacent coefficients equal. Every such constraint is
untestable, and the conclusion depends on the assumption. This work imposes no constraint and
adopts the policy of stating the non-identification explicitly (Section~\ref{sec:apc}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[How it appears in this work]
\label{rem:apc-in-this-work}
The APC problem is latently at work in several places here.

Chapter~\ref{part:industry} treats the time series of $\CCC$; if the age distribution of firms
changes over time, the observed rise in $\CCC$ is a mixture of period and age effects. The
shift-share decomposition of Section~\ref{sec:ccc-decomposition} separates industrial composition
but \textbf{does not separate the age composition}.

If younger firms have shorter $\CCC$, a rising share of older firms could account for the rise in
$\CCC$. This work has not tested that.
\end{remark}

\section{Preference for variance under a deadline}

The proposition of Section~\ref{sec:retiree} is proved here.

\begin{proposition}[Under a deadline, minimizing variance is not optimal]
\label{prop:deadline-variance}
Let $\Theta>0$ be the time required to arrive, $T_{\max}$ the deadline, and define success as
$\{\Theta \leq T_{\max}\}$. When $\E[\Theta] > T_{\max}$, an increase in variance holding
$\E[\Theta]$ fixed can increase the probability of success. In particular the probability of
success converges to $0$ in the limit $\operatorname{Var}[\Theta]\to 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\operatorname{Var}[\Theta]=0$ then $\Theta = \E[\Theta] > T_{\max}$ with probability one, so
$\Prob(\Theta\leq T_{\max})=0$. On the other hand, giving the variable variance while preserving
the mean — say a two-point distribution with $\Theta=T_1<T_{\max}$ with probability $q$ and
$\Theta=T_2$ with probability $1-q$, chosen so that $qT_1+(1-q)T_2=\E[\Theta]$ — gives a success
probability of $q>0$. The increase in variance thus improves the probability of success.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Contrast with the floor constraint]
This proposition does not contradict the conclusion of Section~\ref{sec:bootstrap}, ``lower the
variance with many small amounts''. The latter holds under the floor constraint $M\geq 0$, the
former under the deadline constraint $\Tins \leq E(0)/c_{\rm liv}$. \textbf{Change the type of
constraint and the optimal attitude to variance changes.}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Implications of taking the probability of success as the objective]
\label{rem:deadline-objective}
Proposition~\ref{prop:deadline-variance} takes the objective to be
$\Prob(\Theta\leq T_{\max})$, which differs from $\E[\sum\beta^t\phi_t]$ in
Section~\ref{sec:objective}.

The two diverge because \textbf{the loss from exceeding the deadline does not depend on by how
much it is exceeded}. Missing $T_{\max}$ by a day and by a year give the same result, so the
objective is binary. This discontinuity reverses the attitude to variance.

The insolvency time $\tau$ of Section~\ref{sec:objective} is likewise an absorbing state with the
same property. That is, \textbf{the framework of this work contains regions in which the
objective is not smooth}. The pathwise constraint of Proposition~\ref{prop:pathwise} is a
manifestation of this.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Limits of the proof]
\label{rem:deadline-limit}
The proposition shows that an increase in variance \emph{can} improve the probability of success,
not that it always does. If $\E[\Theta]<T_{\max}$, an increase in variance lowers it instead.

The boundary is the comparison of $\E[\Theta]$ with $T_{\max}$. In practice this becomes the
judgement \textbf{play safe if the prospect suffices, gamble if it does not}, but $\E[\Theta]$ is
not usually knowable in advance. This work does not treat that estimation problem.
\end{remark}

\section{Substitutability of capital and credit}

\begin{proposition}[The substitution relation]
\label{prop:substitution}
When $A=\mathrm{Inv}=0$, the constraint $M\geq 0$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
E \;+\; \sum_i \max\{-\kappa_i,0\} \;\geq\; \sum_i \max\{\kappa_i,0\} .
\end{equation}
That is, $E$ and negative $\kappa$ are perfect substitutes in the constraint.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute $A=\mathrm{Inv}=0$ into equation~\eqref{eq:cash} of Proposition~\ref{prop:cash} and
rearrange $M\geq 0$; the claim follows immediately. The two terms on the left enter with the same
coefficient $1$, so a decrease in one is exactly compensated by an equal increase in the other.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Implications in both directions]
If $E\approx 0$ then $\kappa<0$ is required (Section~\ref{sec:bootstrap}). Conversely, if $E>0$
is large, $\kappa>0$ can be held (Part~\ref{part:results}, the result on implication~F).
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The meaning and the limits of ``perfect substitution''}

\begin{remark}[The substitution holds only in the constraint]
\label{rem:substitution-limit}
Proposition~\ref{prop:substitution} states that $E$ and $-\kappa$ are equivalent
\textbf{only within the single inequality \eqref{eq:cash}}. It does not mean the two are
economically equivalent.

They differ in three respects.

First, \textbf{there is a deadline}. $\kappa<0$ carries an obligation to deliver, and if the
$\tau_i$ of Section~\ref{sec:tau} is finite, delivery equivalent to repayment occurs on the due
date. $E$ has no deadline.

Second, \textbf{availability differs}. $E$ is raised from the capital market, $\kappa<0$ from a
counterparty. As Section~\ref{sec:kappa-literature} sets out, the two are explained by different
theories and become available under different conditions.

Third, \textbf{the claim on $\phi$ differs}. The provider of $E$ holds a residual claim
(Definition~\ref{def:kappa-K}); the provider of $\kappa<0$ does not.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Where the limits of substitution appear]
\label{rem:substitution-boundary}
The one-person business of Chapter~\ref{ch:solo} presupposes $E\approx 0$ and therefore treats
only one endpoint of the substitution.

When $E>0$ was introduced in Section~\ref{sec:retiree}, \textbf{the type of constraint changed
from a floor to a deadline}. This is a manifestation of the asymmetry between $E$ and $\kappa<0$:
$E$ is consumed as living expenses and declines, whereas $\kappa<0$ is not consumed but
extinguished by delivery.

Proposition~\ref{prop:substitution} is thus a claim about a \emph{static} constraint; behaviour
through time differs.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{On reproduction.}
The data obtained and the processing are placed under \texttt{data/}: \texttt{raw/} holds the
inputs as obtained and \texttt{scripts/} the analysis. \texttt{derived/} is intermediate data,
regenerated by \texttt{scripts/parse.py}.

The calculations of Chapter~\ref{part:industry} use \texttt{ccc.py}, \texttt{e1.py},
\texttt{elast.py} and others; the tests of Section~\ref{sec:predY-result} use
\texttt{parse\_jinzai.py} and \texttt{predY.py}. The correspondence is listed in
\texttt{data/README.md}.

\paragraph{On handling.}
All statistics used are public. The Jinzai Service General Site alone, however, publishes
\textbf{the filings of individual offices} rather than aggregates, and this work uses them as
input to statistical analysis.

\textbf{What this work presents is the result of the analysis, not the collection of per-firm data
that was gathered.} That collection is not reproduced, published or redistributed. The repository
contains the processing of the analysis and the aggregates obtained from it. Per-firm data
retrieved from that site — the search-result listings, the office detail pages and the
refund-scheme filings alike — are not included.

What remains is only \textbf{the list of licence and filing-receipt numbers}
(\texttt{data/raw/jinzai/permits.tsv}), which contains none of the filed values. Using it as a
seed, running \texttt{data/scripts/fetch\_jinzai\_detail.py} reproduces the realized fee rates,
turnover rates and placement counts. Retrieval was carried out at six-second intervals per record
and spread across separate days.
