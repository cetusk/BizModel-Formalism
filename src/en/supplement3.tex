\section{The premises imposed in this chapter}

Chapter~\ref{ch:catalog} enumerated the whole space of $\Phi$. This chapter imposes
constraints on it and derives a subspace: which $\Phi$ are feasible under given conditions,
derived from the equations of Part~\ref{part:theory}.

The object is an IT service of a scale one person can run. The premises are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item There is one operator, and elements requiring human coordination are kept to a minimum
\item No outside funding is raised (including crowdfunding)
\item Assets are few and the business starts with no commercial credit
\end{enumerate}

This chapter is deduction, not evidence. The validity of the conclusions depends on the
validity of the premises.

\section{Translating the premises}

\subsection{The financial constraint}

From premises (2) and (3), set $E(0)\approx 0$ and $A\approx 0$. For software,
$\mathrm{Inv}\approx 0$. These are the conditions of Corollary~\ref{cor:zero-equity}, which
requires $\CCC\leq 0$, that is $\sum_i\kappa_i\leq 0$. Written in gross form,

\begin{equation}
\sum_{i} \max\{-\kappa_i,0\} \;\geq\; \sum_{i} \max\{\kappa_i,0\}
\label{eq:solo-finance}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:solo-finance} states that \textbf{the credit drawn must always exceed the
credit extended}. This is not an assumption but the special case of the fundamental
inequality~\eqref{eq:fundamental} with $E=A=0$. It is also the substitution of
Proposition~\ref{prop:substitution} seen at the corner $E=0$.
The assumption $A\approx 0$ agrees with data on capital expenditure by sole proprietors
(Remark~\ref{rem:individual-capex}). Because there is no cushion of equity, the path
constraint of Section~\ref{sec:objective} must hold at every instant.

\subsection{The capacity constraint}

Premise (1) puts a ceiling on total delivery.

\begin{equation}
\sum_{i} \delta_i(t) \;\leq\; \mathrm{Cap}, \qquad \mathrm{Cap} = \text{one person's time}
\label{eq:solo-capacity}
\end{equation}

$\mathrm{Cap}$ is the same quantity that governed the feasibility of memberships in
Proposition~\ref{prop:capacity}. There it was the capacity of facilities on the supply side;
here it is the operator's disposable time. Its value differs by operator and is not
disclosed, so it is not measured in this text.

\subsection{Which one binds}
\label{sec:which-binds}

Equations~\eqref{eq:solo-finance} and~\eqref{eq:solo-capacity} were introduced separately but
are imposed together. Converting both into upper bounds on the scale $r$ makes them
comparable.

\begin{proposition}[The binding constraint switches]
\label{prop:binding-switch}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:rmax}, when $\CCC>0$ the cash constraint gives $r\leq(E-A)/\CCC$.
Converting~\eqref{eq:solo-capacity} into value, the capacity constraint gives
$r\leq v(\mathrm{Cap})$. Hence, with
\begin{equation}
\CCC^{\dagger} = \frac{E-A}{v(\mathrm{Cap})}
\label{eq:ccc-dagger}
\end{equation}
as the boundary, the cash constraint binds where $\CCC>\CCC^{\dagger}$ in~\eqref{eq:ccc-dagger}
and the capacity constraint binds where $\CCC<\CCC^{\dagger}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The smaller of the two bounds binds. $(E-A)/\CCC \leq v(\mathrm{Cap})$ holds exactly when
$\CCC\geq(E-A)/v(\mathrm{Cap})$.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Effort to reduce $\CCC$ increases scale only in the range $\CCC>\CCC^{\dagger}$.}
Below that, capacity binds and changing contractual terms does not increase $r$.

For a solo business with $E\approx 0$, $\CCC^{\dagger}\approx 0$. In Part~\ref{part:theory},
which treated firms, the financial constraint $M(t,\omega)\geq 0$ bound; here the binding
constraint moves to capacity as soon as $\CCC$ is made negative. As customers increase, time
runs out before cash does.
\textbf{That this chapter runs into a capacity constraint after choosing an advance-payment
form is due to this move.}

The design principles therefore come in two.

\section{What the cloud means structurally}

A by-product is the following correspondence.

Before the cloud, procuring servers required capital and $A$ was large. After the cloud
$A\approx 0$, and because usage billing is in arrears, $\kappa_S<0$ arises instead: credit
extended by the infrastructure provider.

\begin{equation}
\text{Effect of the cloud:} \quad A \;\longrightarrow\; \kappa_S<0
\end{equation}

The requirement moved from the physical layer to the credit layer, and its magnitude fell by
orders of magnitude --- from the purchase price of a server to one month's usage fee. That
one person can now start an IT business is explained by this.

\section{The credit bootstrap problem}
\label{sec:bootstrap}

Equation~\eqref{eq:solo-finance} requires $\kappa_C<0$. But being paid in advance requires
credit, and premise (3) says there is none. The contradiction is structural.

There are only three solutions.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \textbf{Make the amount small.} If the absolute amount paid in advance is negligible to
the counterparty, no credit is needed. The consequence is that
\textbf{many small transactions are structurally required}. This is also consistent with the
path constraint of Section~\ref{sec:objective}: dependence on one large customer reaches
$\Tins$ on that customer's single delay.

\item \textbf{Borrow credit.} If a payment platform provides escrow and a refund guarantee,
the customer trusts the platform rather than the operator. Performing 6-2 oneself is
prohibited by regulation, but riding on someone else's is possible. A $\kappa>0$ arises to
the extent of the fee and the payout lag.

\item \textbf{Complete the delivery first.} Sell a finished product. The question of what
supports $M(t)$ during development then remains.
\end{enumerate}

A consequence follows from (c). Writing $T_{\rm dev}$ for the development period,
$T_{\rm acq}$ for the acquisition period, and $c_{\rm liv}$ for the rate of living and
infrastructure costs,

\begin{equation}
E_{\rm required} = c_{\rm liv}\,(T_{\rm dev} + T_{\rm acq})
\label{eq:E-required}
\end{equation}

Under premise (3) this cannot be met, so a route is needed for injecting $E$ from outside.
Salaried income is that route. That is, \textbf{starting while holding another job is not a
matter of attitude but a requirement of the constraint}. Designing $\kappa$ is a question
only after this valley has been crossed.

\subsection{Cash flow of the surviving types}

Annually billed SaaS is the typical case. Both $\kappa_C<0$ (annual prepayment) and
$\kappa_S<0$ (cloud billed in arrears) are negative, so $W<0$ and hence $\CCC<0$, and the
$g^\star$ of~\eqref{eq:gstar} does not exist. Growth generates cash.

Even when financially unconstrained, however, \eqref{eq:solo-capacity} remains. In a design
where the support load is proportional to the number of customers, the capacity constraint
breaks first. In practice this, and not funding, is what stops a solo business.

The design guidance in summary:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Be paid in advance ($\kappa_C<0$)
\item Keep amounts small and numbers large (no credit needed, and it lowers variance under the path constraint)
\item Choose a form whose delivery is not proportional to the number of customers (capacity)
\item Avoid $\Phi$ that the institutional layer prohibits
\item Secure from outside the $E$ needed to cross the valley
\end{enumerate}

Item (3) is the hardest, and failure at (3) despite satisfying (1) and (2) is likely common.

\section{Relaxing a premise: those who hold assets}
\label{sec:retiree}

Relax the asset part of premise (3) and set $E(0)>0$. The case in mind is people who hold
accumulated salary assets after retirement.

\subsection{The social background}

The average age at founding rose from 39.7 in 1979 to 49.7 in 2012. The share of founders
aged 60 and over rose from 19.3\% in 1992 to 35.0\% in 2012 for men, and from 7.2\% to 20.3\%
for women. Teikoku Databank's 2024 survey of newly incorporated companies records an
all-time high average age of 48.4 for company representatives. The premise is realistic.

\subsection{The constraint does not loosen; it changes type}

The effect of $E(0)>0$ is not simply a loosening of the financial constraint. What is
decisive is \textbf{whether $E$ is a flow or a stock}.

\begin{align}
\text{Salaried:}\quad & E(t) = E(0) + (w-c_{\rm liv})\,t \qquad (\text{increasing if } w>c_{\rm liv}) \\
\text{Retired:}\quad & E(t) = E(0) - c_{\rm liv}\,t \qquad\qquad\ \ (\text{monotonically decreasing})
\end{align}

For the salaried, the $M\geq 0$ of~\eqref{eq:constraint} is a \textbf{floor}: on approaching
it one can pull back, and time is an ally. For the retired it is a \textbf{deadline}. With no
revenue the insolvency time is bounded above deterministically by

\begin{equation}
\Tins \;\leq\; \frac{E(0)}{c_{\rm liv}}
\label{eq:deadline}
\end{equation}

and arrives regardless of the choice of $\Phi$.

Proposition~\ref{prop:deadline-variance} supplies this.

\begin{proposition}[Preference over variance under a deadline]
Under the deadline constraint~\eqref{eq:deadline}, a strategy that lowers variance is not
necessarily optimal. If the expected time to completion of the safe route exceeds
$E(0)/c_{\rm liv}$, that route's probability of success is nearly zero.
\end{proposition}

The advice ``keep amounts small and numbers large to reduce variance'' derived in
Section~\ref{sec:bootstrap}(a) was a conclusion under a floor constraint. It can reverse when
the type of constraint changes.

There is a more important bifurcation.

The bifurcation is also observed across age groups. Analyses of the labour economy by the

\subsection{The change in the objective can be expressed through parameters}
\label{sec:r2-sst}

The observed change in motives can be written either as a change in the \emph{form} of the
objective or as a change in its \emph{parameters}. The former is the stronger claim.

Socioemotional selectivity theory (\cite{carstensen1999}) takes the latter. It holds that as
time horizon contracts, goals shift from acquiring knowledge to emotional meaning, and that
what drives the shift is not age itself but \textbf{perceived remaining time}. Experimentally
inducing a limited time horizon in younger people reportedly produces preference patterns
close to those of older people.

Translated into the formulation of Section~\ref{sec:objective}, what changes is $H$ and
$\beta$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
$H \to$ small & fewer terms in the sum; distant future $\phi_t$ do not enter the objective \\
$\beta \to$ small & the future is weighted less; the present state is valued relatively more \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\captionof{table}{Effect of a contracting time horizon on the objective: what changes in $H$ and $\beta$.}
\label{tab:solo-h-beta}
\end{center}

In the limit of both, the objective approaches an evaluation of the present state itself
rather than of accumulated future $\phi$. That is what the theory means by prioritizing
present-oriented emotional goals over future-oriented ones.

\textbf{This formulation requires no rewriting of the objective.} The framework of
Section~\ref{sec:objective} is used unchanged.

\begin{remark}[Why a formulation that changes the form of the objective is not adopted]
\label{rem:phi-demotion-retracted}
R2 could be written as $\max(\text{some other objective})$ subject to $\phi\geq 0$, moving
$\phi$ from the objective into a constraint. That is not adopted here, for two reasons.

First, the grounds are insufficient. What is observed is the distribution of stated motives,
which says nothing about the \emph{form} of the objective. Deriving a structural change from
a distribution of motives is a leap.

Second, and more seriously, \textbf{$\max(\text{some other objective})$ subject to
$\phi\geq 0$ has no content}. Unless ``some other objective'' is specified, the formulation
can explain any behaviour and therefore predicts nothing. It has the same defect as the one
noted for the measurability hypothesis in Section~\ref{sec:predB-illposed}.

Written as changes in $H$ and $\beta$, the form of the objective is preserved, and perceived
remaining time even has a measurement scale (Future Time Perspective).
\textbf{Where the same observation can be explained under a weaker assumption, that is the
one to take.}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The state of the theory]
Socioemotional selectivity theory is itself contested. There are reports that a limited
future time horizon does not necessarily contribute to emotional well-being and can predict
worse well-being, and the structure of the scale itself is being reconsidered.
This text does not adopt the theory; it only notes that
\textbf{a route exists that explains the observation without changing the form of the
objective}.
\end{remark}
It has also been noted that founders in their fifties frequently cite securing income, while
those in their sixties more often seek connection with society.

\subsection{Family 3 revives, and a reconciliation with data}

In the sieve, family 3 (renting assets over time) dropped out entirely for requiring $A$.
Introducing $E(0)>0$ should revive it.

By field of business at founding by age in the 2014 White Paper on Small and Medium
Enterprises, among those aged 60 and over the leading fields are services 39.1\%, real estate

A sixtyfold difference. The deduction that the presence of $E(0)$ decides the availability of
family 3 agrees with the distribution by age. This is an after-the-fact reconciliation and
shows no causation, but because the sieve was stated in advance it has value as a check.

\subsection{Restating the path constraint}

The constraint of Section~\ref{sec:objective} has been written $M(t)\geq 0$, which is
inappropriate for this group.

\begin{equation}
M(t,\omega) \;\geq\; M_{\rm floor}, \qquad M_{\rm floor} \gg 0
\end{equation}

$E(0)$ cannot be re-earned through labour. Even for an identical loss, the asymmetry of
utility differs from that of the working-age population. In practice the design is to carve
out in advance a business budget $B$ that may be spent and leave $E(0)-B$ untouched. The R2
trajectory of Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory} halts at a lower bound where this discipline
operates.

Note that support services and information aimed at this group face an incentive to have
$E$ spent beyond $B$. Fixing $M_{\rm floor}$ in advance is not a modelling device but a
practical defence.

\section{The formation of credit}
\label{sec:credit-formation}

\subsection{Why credit attributable to an individual is zero}

Independently of $E$, consider whether a retiree can bring credit with them. The conclusion
is $S_{\rm ind}\approx 0$, and this is not a rule of thumb but a consequence of the
measurability condition of Chapter~\ref{sec:info}.

Credit is the ground on which a counterparty accepts $\kappa_C<0$, that is, a verifiable
record of performance, and it must be in $\mathcal{G}$. But \textbf{an individual's
contribution inside an organization is by definition not measurable from outside the
organization}. This is no accident: it is precisely because it is not measurable that a
principal--agent problem exists inside the organization.

\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\text{the organization is observed as the performing party}\\
\Rightarrow\; \text{the record of performance attaches to the organization}\\
\Rightarrow\; \text{the individual's contribution is outside } \mathcal{G}
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

An organization is a device that absorbs measurability from outside, and losing that on
departure is the natural consequence of having enjoyed it.

\begin{remark}[Not a sale but original attribution]
Saying that credit is ``continuously sold'' during employment is inaccurate. A sale implies
transferring something once held.

Just as the author of a work made for hire is the legal person from the outset, credit
\textbf{attaches originally to the organization at the moment it arises}. It does not arise
with the individual and then transfer.

The difference reaches practice. If it were a sale, one could choose to stop selling; with
original attribution, \textbf{the terms of attribution themselves must be changed}.
Publishing under one's own name while employed is an operation that changes where credit
attaches.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Separating three quantities}

What becomes discontinuous on leaving employment is settled once the quantities are
separated.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXl@{}}
\toprule
Quantity & Content & On leaving \\
\midrule
Usable credit & the degree to which counterparties actually accept advance payment & discontinuous downwards \\
Held credit $S_{\rm ind}$ & what is in $\mathcal{G}$ under one's own name & continuous, remains near zero \\
Convertible stock & personal networks, technical knowledge & continuous, but decays thereafter \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\captionof{table}{Three quantities that behave differently around departure.}
\label{tab:solo-credit-decomposition}
\end{center}

While employed, an individual \emph{borrows} the organization's credit rather than holding
it. What falls on departure is the return of the borrowed part; the held part was zero to
begin with and does not change.
\textbf{It looks discontinuous, but what is lost was never one's own.}

\begin{remark}[Leaving employment is an act of measurement]
While employed, the borrowed and the held parts are bundled and cannot be separated. Leaving
is an act of measurement that makes visible a decomposition previously outside $\mathcal{G}$.
Nobody can tell how much credit was one's own until the organization is removed. That it
cannot be estimated in advance is not a lack of information but the fact that it has, in
principle, never been measured.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Two discontinuities, and a lag between them}

Not everything falls on departure. During employment, non-compete and confidentiality
obligations restrict publication, and leaving removes them:
\textbf{the freedom to convert is discontinuous upwards}. But non-compete obligations run
for a period and in most cases do not lift immediately.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\insertfig{credit-formation}
\caption{The three quantities around departure. The two discontinuities point in opposite directions and are separated in time.}
\label{fig:credit-jump}
\end{figure}

\begin{remark}[What is at a maximum is not credit]
The statement ``one's credit assets are at a maximum just after leaving'' is wrong. Credit is
zero, and remains zero after leaving.

What is at a maximum is the \textbf{convertible stock}: contacts and technical knowledge.
That is not credit but material that can be converted into credit through the operation of
publishing. Properly stated, what is at a maximum is not credit but the stock of
convertibility, and it decays thereafter as $e^{-\lambda t}$.

The conclusion --- move early --- is unchanged, but the reason differs. It is not that a held
asset is depleting but that \textbf{the material for turning something into an asset is
depleting}.
\end{remark}

The shaded region of Figure~\ref{fig:credit-jump} is the structural problem. The non-compete
period coincides with the period in which the stock decays fastest, and conversion is
impossible during it. The higher the decay rate $\lambda$ in a field, the greater the loss.

The only remedy for this lag is to produce results under one's own name while still employed.
Structurally, remedies after leaving arrive too late.

\subsection{Capital and credit are substitutes}

Restating~\eqref{eq:solo-finance}: when $E\approx 0$, one has no choice but to earn
$\sum\max\{-\kappa_i,0\}$, and \textbf{credit is the only means of funding}. If $E>0$, the
inequality holds even without that term.

This is the substitution of Proposition~\ref{prop:substitution} seen at this chapter's corner
$E\approx 0$. Hence \textbf{a retiree's advantage is capital, not credit}. In the dimension
of credit they start from zero, like a young founder without assets.

\begin{remark}[Confusing experience with credit]
Popular accounts of founding a business later in life repeatedly cite experience, skills and
contacts. But \textbf{experience and credit are different things}. Experience lowers
$C_{\mathrm{prod}}$; what moves the sign of $\kappa$ is credit. Experience that cannot be
verified from outside does not obtain advance payment.

Confusing the two obscures the correct strategy of using capital in place of credit, and
leads instead to the wrong question: ``why is there no work despite the experience?''
\end{remark}

\subsection{Starting with the sign reversed}

The guidance of Section~\ref{sec:bootstrap} required $\kappa_C<0$, which cannot be executed
initially under the premise of zero credit. The following trajectory is taken instead.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
Early & accept $\kappa\geq 0$ (allow payment in arrears and bridge with capital) \\
Middle & publish and accumulate each engagement in a form that enters $\mathcal{G}$ \\
Late & shift to $\kappa<0$ (advance payment becomes obtainable) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\captionof{table}{The trajectory from zero credit to advance payment, in three stages.}
\label{tab:solo-credit-trajectory}
\end{center}

\textbf{The role of capital is to buy the period in which credit is built.}
Rewriting~\eqref{eq:E-required},

\begin{equation}
E_{\rm required} = c_{\rm liv}\,(T_{\rm dev} + T_{\rm acq} + T_{\rm credit})
\label{eq:E-required-2}
\end{equation}

adds the term $T_{\rm credit}$. Without assets this term cannot be covered, which is why
credit had to be borrowed from a platform as in Section~\ref{sec:bootstrap}(b). With capital
it can be built without borrowing. That is the concrete content of the substitution.

\section{The trade-off between contract work and publishing}

\subsection{The conflict is not peculiar to individuals; the inability to parallelize is}

Contract work is the efficient way to obtain assets; publishing results is what is needed to
obtain credit efficiently. Organizations face the same conflict: development contractors and
consulting firms alike find it hard to accumulate a public record because of confidentiality.

The difference lies in the form of the capacity constraint.

\begin{align}
\text{Organization:}\quad & \sum\delta \leq \mathrm{Cap}, \quad \mathrm{Cap}\ \text{proportional to headcount} \\
\text{Individual:}\quad & \delta_{\text{contract}} + \delta_{\text{public}} \leq \mathrm{Cap}, \quad \mathrm{Cap}\ \text{fixed}
\end{align}

An organization can run both in parallel and cross-subsidize. It performs family 7-2
(cross-subsidy) not between external customers but between internal activities. An individual
has no such means.
\textbf{What is peculiar to the individual is not the conflict but the inability to
parallelize.}

\subsection{Partial conversion and the optimal allocation}

Even when aiming at the upper right, the substitution is not one for one. Writing $\Ccl$ for
the part of the $\mathrm{Cap}$ of~\eqref{eq:solo-capacity} devoted to contract work and
$\mathrm{Cap}-\Ccl$ for the part devoted to publishing,

\begin{equation}
\text{accumulation of public credit} = \gamma\,(\mathrm{Cap}-\Ccl) + \theta\,\Ccl,
\qquad 0<\theta<\gamma
\label{eq:leakage}
\end{equation}

The second term is the leakage. Of the problems solved on a client engagement,
\textbf{the part that generalizes can be published}. The engagement itself cannot be written
about, but the general technical problem encountered in it can. And because the thinking is
already done, the marginal cost of writing is low.

Equation~\eqref{eq:leakage} is linear in $\Ccl$ and, since $0<\theta<\gamma$, strictly
decreasing. On its own this makes the optimal allocation $\Ccl=0$, that is, no contract work
at all. The allocation is settled at an interior value only because contract work brings in
cash.

\begin{proposition}[Contract work stays at the minimum the constraint requires]
\label{prop:allocation}
Let $w$ be the hourly return on contract work and $c_{\rm liv}$ the rate of living costs.
The cash required by the fundamental inequality~\eqref{eq:fundamental} is
$w\,\Ccl\geq c_{\rm liv}$, and the allocation maximizing~\eqref{eq:leakage} is
\begin{equation}
\Ccl^{\ast} = \frac{c_{\rm liv}}{w}
\label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The objective is strictly decreasing in $\Ccl$ and the feasible set is
$\Ccl\geq c_{\rm liv}/w$, so the smallest feasible $\Ccl$ is optimal.
\end{proof}

\textbf{The optimum is not an interior point of the objective but the edge of the constraint.}
If $E(0)>0$ the constraint loosens to $w\,\Ccl\geq c_{\rm liv} - E(0)/T$ and $\Ccl^\ast$
falls. The statement in Section~\ref{sec:retiree} that capital buys the period in which
credit is built can be written as a fall in the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:allocation}.

Separately from the allocation, there is room to raise $\theta$.
\textbf{The criterion for choosing engagements moves from the fee to $\theta$.}
At equal rates, choose the engagement containing a generalizable problem. The wall of
confidentiality is cleared at the level of abstraction, not at the level of the engagement.

\section{Employment contracts reread}

From this chapter's argument, an employment contract can be written as follows.

\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\text{the individual supplies } \delta \;\to\; \text{credit attaches to the organization}\\
\to\; \text{the individual receives stability of } \kappa_L
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\textbf{An employment contract is a trade in which stability of $\kappa$ is bought at the
price of placing the attribution of credit with the organization.} In exchange for a certain
monthly receipt, the credit asset accumulated does not become one's own.

This has the same form as the argument about discount factors in
Section~\ref{sec:objective}, and the same structure as the choice between families 5-1 and
1-3 discussed in Chapter~\ref{ch:catalog}.

The terms of attribution are a negotiable variable. Securing what may be produced under one's
own name while employed --- public talks, contributions to public repositories, writing ---
belongs to the same dimension as negotiating pay. In many cases it is granted at no cost.

\section{Limits of this chapter}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{This is deduction, not evidence.}
Apart from the reconciliation of family 3's revival with the distribution by age, the
conclusions of this chapter are deductions from premises. If the premises are wrong, so are
the conclusions.

\item \textbf{$S_{\rm ind}\approx 0$ depends on the field.}
In fields that are small enough that participants know one another as individuals ---
academic disciplines, particular technical communities, small professions --- an individual's
performance is observed without passing through an organization. There the separation of the
borrowed from the held part is already advanced during employment, and the discontinuity on
departure is small. The size of $S_{\rm ind}$ depends not only on individual effort but on
\textbf{the measurability structure of the field}.

\item \textbf{The objective under R2 is not identified.}
It was shown that the change can be written through $H$ and $\beta$, but not verified that
this is sufficient. Strictly, therefore, no claim of optimality under R2 has been made.

\item \textbf{The decay rate $\lambda$ of the convertible stock is not measured.}
How serious the lag of Figure~\ref{fig:credit-jump} is in practice depends on $\lambda$, and
there is no basis here for its value.
\end{enumerate}
