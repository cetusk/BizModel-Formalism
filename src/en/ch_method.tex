\chapter{Selection Bias: Decomposing the Distortions That Enter Observation}
\label{ch:method}

\section{The problem}

Applying the framework of Part~\ref{part:theory} to real firms introduces systematic
distortion at the very start of any comparison. The distortion is not a single ``bias'' but
a mixture of at least eight problems of different kinds. They are decomposed below, and
those that can be handled are separated from those that cannot be, in principle.

\section{Conditioning on survival: a circularity in the framework}
\label{sec:survivorship}

An observable firm is the realization of a path that did not violate the
constraint~\eqref{eq:constraint}. Writing the insolvency time as
$\tau=\inf\{t:M(t)<0\}$, what is observed is
\begin{equation}
\Prob(X \mid \tau > t)
\end{equation}
and not $\Prob(X)$. And what fixes $\tau$ is precisely $\Phi$, the object of estimation.
The structure is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:dag}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\insertfig{collider}
\caption{Survival $S$ is a common consequence of $\Phi$ and $\varepsilon$ --- a collider. Conditioning on $S$ induces a spurious correlation between them.}
\label{fig:dag}
\end{figure}

$S$ is a common consequence of $\Phi$ and the exogenous shock $\varepsilon$, that is, a
collider. Conditioning on a collider induces correlation between two causes that are
independent (Berkson's paradox).

\subsection{The direction of the distortion reverses with what is conditioned on}

The distinction matters in practice.

\paragraph{Conditioning on survival.}
Among surviving firms, those that persisted despite an unfavourable $\Phi$ are those whose
$\varepsilon$ was favourable. In a surviving sample the disadvantage of $\Phi$ therefore
appears offset by an advantage in $\varepsilon$, and \textbf{the effect of structure is
understated}. The disadvantage of $\CCC>0$ looks smaller than it is when only survivors are
examined.

\paragraph{Conditioning on success (fame).}
Here the situation differs. Collecting only successful firms removes from the sample every
firm that failed with the same $\Phi$, so there is no control group. The problem is not
understatement but that \textbf{estimation is impossible at all}: the sample contains no
information that separates $\Phi$ from $\varepsilon$.

Treating both as one and the same ``survivorship bias'' hides this difference.

\section{Selection into disclosure: correlated with the axis of interest}
\label{sec:disclosure}

Listing and disclosure are not random selections. By~\eqref{eq:gstar}, a firm with $\CCC<0$
has no $g^\star$ and can finance growth internally, so in principle it has little need to go
to outside capital markets. Conversely a firm with a large positive $\CCC$ needs outside
capital and is pushed towards listing.

That is, \textbf{the probability of selection is itself a function of the object of
estimation}. The selection probability $\Prob(S_{\mathrm{disc}}=1 \mid \CCC)$ tends to be
increasing in $\CCC$. The distribution of $\CCC$ observed in a database of listed companies
is therefore displaced from the population distribution systematically and in a predictable
direction.

\section{Age: a problem of domain, not of bias}
\label{sec:age}

That a firm on its first day cannot be compared with one several years old is a problem of a
different kind. Equation~\eqref{eq:ccc} was derived assuming a steady state and is undefined
at $r\approx 0$, where it divides by zero.

This is therefore not a bias but \textbf{a question of the framework's range of application}.
The remedy differs accordingly: a bias is corrected, whereas a question of domain can only be
declared. Concretely, one of the following is taken.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Restrict the objects to a region where $r$ is stable, and state the threshold.
\item Model the transient explicitly with a separate system of variables.
\item Lower the unit of analysis from the firm to $\Phi$ and judge maturity per $\Phi$ (Section~\ref{sec:unit}).
\end{enumerate}

Introducing a correction factor and forcing the steady-state formula onto the data is the
remedy most to be avoided.

\section{Age, period and cohort are not identified}
\label{sec:apc}

Three clocks act on an observed firm at once. Writing $c$ for the founding cohort, $p$ for
the year of observation and $a$ for age (this section follows the notation of the
Age--Period--Cohort literature; $a,p,c$ have these meanings in this section only and are
unrelated to their use elsewhere),
\begin{equation}
a = p - c
\label{eq:apc-main}
\end{equation}
holds identically. By~\eqref{eq:apc-main} the three variables are linearly dependent, and
\textbf{identifying the three effects simultaneously is impossible without adding an
exogenous restriction}. This has been discussed at length in demography and epidemiology as
the Age--Period--Cohort problem, and no general solution exists.

Hence the question ``is a given industry's favourable $\CCC$ in a given period due to
$\Phi$, to youth, or to the financial environment of the time?'' cannot be answered from data
alone. All one can do is state an identifying assumption --- setting one of the effects to
zero, say --- and show how much the conclusion depends on it.

\section{Retrospective narrative}
\label{sec:narrative}

The description of a successful firm's $\Phi$ is itself contaminated. The structure is
retold so as to explain the success, so bias enters the \emph{measurement} of the
explanatory variable $\Phi$. No correction on the dependent-variable side removes it.

As Part~\ref{part:theory} stated, most $\Phi$ are not products of design but arise as
properties of a line of business, their financial function recognized after the fact.
Distinguishing primary sources --- contemporaneous disclosures, contracts, reporting ---
from retrospective accounts is the minimum remedy.

\section{Mistaking the unit: what a row denotes versus what its values denote}
\label{sec:misidentification}

The preceding sections concerned distortions in the \emph{selection} of the sample. Failures
of a different kind occur at the stage of acquisition.

\textbf{What a row denotes and what the values in that row denote need not coincide.}
When a published list has rows at a lower unit while the figures are reported at a higher
unit, the value is replicated across every row belonging to the same higher unit. Treating
the list as observations lets an object with many lower units count as many times over.

\textbf{Missing values can be detected in the output; this mistake cannot.} The values are
complete, so it passes any post-collection check. Section~\ref{sec:predY-result} records a
case in which it reversed the conclusion.

There is only one remedy: \textbf{confirm the unit at which the values are reported and
consolidate at that unit}. Consolidation is not preprocessing; it is a step that decides the
conclusion.

\section{Selection of variables: the gaps are not random}
\label{sec:variable-selection}

The foregoing concerned distortions in the sample and in identification. The same kind of
problem arises for \emph{which variables are observable}.

There can be a correlation whereby the areas of greatest theoretical interest are the hardest
to measure. A field in which institutions have intervened heavily is evidence that the
authorities judged there to be a problem there, and theoretical interest is drawn to the same
place. Yet in a field whose transaction forms are unstable enough to require intervention,
that very instability can make the required quantity impossible to compute from published
aggregates (Remark~\ref{rem:data-gap-relevance}).

\textbf{Treating gaps in the data as random systematically removes from the sample the area
one most wants to know about.} The other sections of this chapter treat selection of the
sample; this is selection of variables, and no correction on the sample side addresses it.
All one can do is describe where the gaps are concentrated.

before a survey's prospects can be assessed.}

\section{How to proceed}

What can be handled is listed separately from what cannot.

\subsection{Declare the frame in advance and track forward}

Rather than ``collect well-known firms'', define ``all operators in a given industry
existing at a reference date $t_0$'' and track forward from there. Survival then becomes an
observed dependent variable rather than a hidden filter, and the collider conditioning of
Section~\ref{sec:survivorship} is avoided.

Available frames in Japan include the Economic Census and the databases of Teikoku Databank
and Tokyo Shoko Research. In Europe, Orbis covers unlisted firms broadly. Disclosure
obligations in the United States are looser, so its unlisted data are relatively weak; in
this respect Japan and Europe are better placed.

\subsection{Collect the failure side explicitly}

Insolvencies are observable through the official gazette and the tabulations of credit
research firms. If a denominator can be constructed, survivorship bias becomes an estimable
selection model.

\subsection{Lower the unit of analysis from the firm to \texorpdfstring{$\Phi$}{Phi}}
\label{sec:unit}

This is the most effective remedy. By the definitions of Chapter~\ref{sec:phi}, $\Phi$ is an
object at the level of a contract and a firm is a bundle of them. A large firm normally runs
several $\Phi$ in parallel whose $\kappa$ differ even in sign. Computing $\CCC$ at the level
of the firm is looking at an average that mixes them.

Lowering the unit of analysis to $\Phi$
\begin{itemize}
\item partially resolves the age problem (a new $\Phi$ inside a mature firm can be handled)
\item increases the number of observations
\item reduces the influence of the institutional artefact called ``the firm''
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Group by the shape of the constraint, not by industry classification}

Industry classifications fix a particular era's view of industry and differ by country.
Grouping instead by the structural features the framework of Part~\ref{part:theory} names ---
\begin{itemize}
\item presence of a capacity constraint (Section~\ref{sec:breakage})
\item transaction frequency $\nu$ (\eqref{eq:decay})
\item observability of effort (Chapter~\ref{sec:info})
\item capital intensity (the weight of $A$ in~\eqref{eq:cash})
\end{itemize}
makes comparison meaningful across industries and eras. This is the return on having
constructed the theory.

\subsection{Where a distortion cannot be removed, state its sign}

The non-identification of Section~\ref{sec:apc} and the retrospective narrative of
Section~\ref{sec:narrative} cannot be removed. Their direction can still be stated. Saying
``this estimate understates the effect of structure'' or ``this description of $\Phi$ likely
contains after-the-fact rationalization'' makes the robustness of a conclusion assessable.

\subsection{A rule for using well-known firms}

Hence the following rule.
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Well-known firms are used for illustration, not for inference.}
\end{quote}
Citing an individual firm to explain a structure is permitted. Concluding that a structure is
superior from the existence of a successful example is not.
